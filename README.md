Newsroom Nine
Nine minutes. Fact check claim. Your call.
Newsroom Nine is a free, browser-based verification game for journalists, editors, students and anyone who shares things online. 
A viral claim lands on your desk. You have a clock, a limited number of checks and a decision to make: publish it, hold it, or debunk it. 
When you finish, a debrief shows what you caught, what you missed and which free tools to use next time.
Play it: https://programmer-eng-droid.github.io/newsroom-nine/
What you do in a case
1.	Setup: choose a track (learner or professional), your medium (print, TV, radio or online) and your role.
2.	Access: run checks against the clock. You cannot afford all of them, so choose wisely. Results land on an evidence board.
3.	Analyse: rate how much each finding really proves.
4.	Reflect: check your own bias before deciding.
5.	Create and Act: write the line you would publish and decide what goes out.
6.	Debrief: see your score by skill, the tells in the rumour, and real tools to use next time.
The learner track gives hints and short explanations along the way. The professional track has a tighter clock and explains everything at the end. Your score out of 100 is based on your process as well as your final decision. A certificate of participation can be printed or saved as a PDF.
Who it is for
•	Working reporters, sub-editors, news editors, producers and social media editors
•	Journalism students and trainers
•	Anyone who wants to practise checking a claim before sharing it
Works on phone and computer
•	Open the link in any modern browser. Nothing to install.
•	Install it as an app: on Android tap Install app; on iPhone tap Share, then Add to Home Screen; on a computer use the install icon in the address bar.
•	Works offline after the first visit, which helps when data is expensive or the signal is weak.
Privacy
No accounts, no tracking and no data sent anywhere. Your best score is saved only on your own device.
Practice scenarios are fictional
All places, people, organisations and events in the cases are invented for training. This is a practice exercise, not an accredited qualification.
Status
Early prototype with one case ("The bridge photo"). More cases, local scenarios and a facilitator (team) mode are planned. Scoring values and sample wording are placeholders and should be reviewed by working journalists and fact-checkers before wider use.
